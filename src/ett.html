Exercise and medication stress testing

Stress Tests

Stress tests help your care team see how your heart responds to exercise or medication. Your appointment letter or care team will tell you which type of test you are booked for.

Stress Test Types

  • Exercise/Treadmill Stress Test
  • Exercise Myocardial Perfusion Imaging (MIBI)
  • Persantine MIBI Stress Test
  • Dobutamine MIBI Stress Test

Before the Test

  • Wear comfortable clothing and shoes if you may be exercising.
  • Ten stickers called electrodes will be placed on your chest to monitor your heart rhythm.
  • Clothing from the waist up may need to be removed for electrode placement. You will be provided with a gown.
  • Small areas of body hair may be shaved if needed so the stickers make good contact.
  • Your test is monitored by technologists and supervised by the clinic physician.

Exercise/Treadmill Stress Test

You will walk or jog on a treadmill that gets faster and steeper every 3 minutes. The goal is to raise your heart rate to a target range.

Exercise Myocardial Perfusion Imaging (MIBI)

This is a treadmill stress test with an imaging component. Once your target heart rate is reached, a small amount of radioactive tracer is injected into a vein.

  • You continue walking for about one minute after the injection.
  • After a short recovery, you go to Nuclear Medicine for pictures of your heart.
  • If you cannot reach the target heart rate, your care team may switch to another stress-test method when appropriate.

Persantine MIBI Stress Test

Persantine simulates exercise by increasing blood flow to the heart. This test may be used when a treadmill test is not suitable.

  • You will be lying down throughout the test.
  • The medication is given through an IV line over 4 minutes.
  • Tell the technologist if you have asthma or another breathing condition.
  • Avoid caffeine for 24 hours before this test unless your care team gives different instructions. This includes coffee, tea, energy drinks, and chocolate.
  • Possible side effects include headache, chest discomfort, flushing, or warmth. Medication is available if symptoms are severe.

Dobutamine MIBI Stress Test

Dobutamine stimulates the heart to beat faster and more strongly, similar to exercise. It may be used when exercise or Persantine is not appropriate.

  • You will be lying down throughout the test.
  • The medication is given gradually through an IV line.
  • Your heart rate, blood pressure, and ECG are monitored closely.
  • Tell the technologist if you have a history of heart rhythm abnormalities.
  • Possible side effects include palpitations, chest discomfort, headache, or nausea.
